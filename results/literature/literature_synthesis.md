# Reading the genome off the slide: where this project stands against the literature

## MSI from H&E: a settled field

The MSI-from-H&E lineage has moved from proof of concept to CE-marked product in seven years, and the project's numbers sit at the 2019 end of that arc. The founding paper reported patient-level AUROC 0.77 on TCGA-CRC-DX, 0.84 on TCGA-CRC-KR and 0.81 on TCGA-STAD, with 0.84 on the external DACHS cohort of 378 patients ([Kather et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-019-0462-y)). One year later the same group pooled 8,836 colorectal tumours and reached AUROC 0.92 in cross-validation and 0.95–0.96 in an external cohort of 771 specimens, explicitly claiming clinical grade ([Echle et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1053/j.gastro.2020.06.021)). Subsequent work tightened this further: 0.909 internally and 0.900 on 805 external patients with annotation-free training ([Schrammen et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1002/path.5800)), sensitivity 0.99 with NPV above 0.99 across 16 cohorts and more than 13,000 patients ([Wagner et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ccell.2023.08.002)), and a blind validation of the CE-IVD tool MSIntuit on 600 consecutive patients at sensitivity 0.96–0.98 and specificity 0.47 ([Saillard et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-023-42453-6)). Against that, a development cross-validation of ~0.765 and a locked TCGA test of 0.76–0.83 is a faithful reproduction of 2019, not a result the field would read as new.

The CPTAC-COAD number is the one most exposed. A spatially aware graph neural network trained on TCGA-COAD was externally validated on 161 CPTAC-COAD slides four years ago and reported MSI AUROC 73.15 there, alongside mutation AUCs of 63.74–82.90 ([Ding et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2589-7500%2822%2900168-6)). An external AUROC of 0.70–0.79 on the same cohort is therefore a replication of a published result, and the honest framing is as a reproducibility check. The degradation itself is expected: pooled deep-learning MSI specificity falls from 0.86 internally to 0.71 externally across the published corpus, which the authors read as overfitting ([Li et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41746-025-01848-z)).

The closest methodological twin appeared in 2026 and uses the same encoder family: frozen Phikon embeddings with mean/max pooling under patient-level cross-validation, reporting pooled ROC-AUC 0.913 after nested calibration but 0.788 in a source-split holdout, and closing with a call for independent multicentre validation against clinically adjudicated endpoints ([Özkan Vardar & Vardar 2026](https://doi.org/10.3390/diagnostics16203271)).

## The image-to-protein claim

No retrieved study links an H&E-derived MSI or MMR image score to mass-spectrometry-measured MMR protein abundance in the same patients. The nearest precedent states in its own introduction that there had been no prior attempt to correlate rich proteomic data with histologic features, and covers CCRCC, HNSCC, LSCC, LUAD, pancreatic and endometrial cancer across 2,755 slides from 657 CPTAC patients — with no colorectal cohort and a single passing mention of MSI ([Wang et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.xcrm.2023.101173)). Protein levels have been inferred directly from unannotated slides in breast cancer using CPTAC-BRCA as the protein cohort ([Liu et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41523-024-00620-y)), and CPTAC image–proteomics mining goes back to kidney cancer ([Azuaje et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.3390/jcm8101535)). So the method is established and the specific pairing — image MSI score against MSH2/MSH6 abundance in CPTAC-COAD — is not. This is the project's most defensible novelty, provided it is stated that narrowly.

## Site signal and TCGA contamination

Submitting-site signatures are detectable in TCGA histology across more than 3,000 patients, survive colour normalisation and augmentation, bias survival, mutation and stage prediction, and even encode ethnicity; site-preserved splits were proposed as the fix ([Howard et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-021-24698-1)), which is exactly what hospital-grouped cross-validation implements. The foundation-model era has made this sharper: all twenty models in a public robustness benchmark show deficits to non-biological features severe enough to cause diagnostic errors ([Kömen et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-73923-2)), ten public encoders were found to represent the medical centre strongly with only one achieving a robustness index above one ([arXiv:2501.18055](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.18055), preprint), and an audit of TCGA-derived multimodal benchmarks documented case-level train/test overlap of 92.3–100% with near-complete tissue-source-site overlap, both linearly decodable from foundation-model features ([arXiv:2607.12278](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12278), preprint). A 2026 review names data overlap and benchmark contamination outright as translation barriers ([Zhang et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.34133/research.1332)). For MSI specifically, UNI led TCGA cross-validation at 0.778 balanced accuracy but fell to 0.764 externally while CONCH generalised better, and a fine-tuned ResNet34 beat every foundation model internally at 0.836 ([Bilal et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compmedimag.2025.102680)).

## Human and LLM readers

The pathologist-reading-MSI tradition predates the models: MsPath reached 93% sensitivity and 55% specificity in 1,098 population-based cancers ([Jenkins et al. 2007](https://doi.org/10.1053/j.gastro.2007.04.044)) and logistic models on 1,649 cancers classified MSI-H correctly about 85% of the time ([Greenson et al. 2009](https://doi.org/10.1097/PAS.0b013e31817ec2b1)). The decisive comparison remains five gastrointestinal pathologists reading 40 balanced TCGA cases at mean AUROC 0.605 against a model at 0.865 ([Yamashita et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/S1470-2045%2820%2930535-0)). Those same hand-scored features, automated, now match black-box models at AUC 0.88–0.90 with full interpretability ([Baumann et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.modpat.2025.100932)). No retrieved study pits an LLM or VLM reader against an encoder-plus-MIL pipeline on a molecular endpoint; the GPT-4V work is on morphologic tasks ([Ferber et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-51465-9)).

## TP53 and CDH1: a thin literature

TP53-from-H&E has been reported at least fourteen times since 2018, but only five studies yield a verifiable TP53-specific point AUROC (median 0.75, range 0.63–0.82), and exactly one reports a TP53 AUROC measured at a different institution: 0.572 in gastric cancer, down from 0.727 on TCGA ([Jang et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.3748/wjg.v27.i44.7687)). CDH1 is thinner still — that same paper is the only retrieved gastric CDH1 genotype classifier (TCGA AUC 0.842 frozen, 0.781 FFPE, falling to 0.667 externally), and it reports no Lauren-histotype comparator. The only study that interrogates whether a CDH1 classifier exceeds histotype is in breast, where CDH1-mutation accuracy (0.95) and lobular-histotype accuracy (0.96) are indistinguishable, the added value coming from alternative CDH1 inactivation found in 74% of AI-positive, mutation-negative cases ([Pareja et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1158/0008-5472.CAN-24-1322)). Meanwhile diffuse-type and signet-ring morphology are near-saturated at AUC 0.95–0.99 ([Kanavati & Tsuneki 2021](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-021-99940-3)), and a deep-learning Lauren call already out-stratifies the pathologist's ([Veldhuizen et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10120-023-01398-x)). A gastric CDH1 model that beats a histotype baseline has not been published — and the matching caution is that in colorectal multi-target models, the high-AUROC biomarkers largely correlate with MSI morphology ([Gustav et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.landig.2025.100891)).
